# Slides

Nine decks: one per class, plus a volunteer briefing for the prep session.

## The eight class decks

Built to be shown to the students on the projector — not to be read by the volunteer. Big type,
one idea per slide, and every code example drawn as real Scratch blocks in their real palette
colours, so a seven-year-old who can't yet read the handout can still match a colour and a shape
on screen.

**The teaching detail is in the speaker notes, not on the slides.** Segment timings, the
deliberate-mistake script, the common-bugs table and the notes for the second volunteer all sit in
the notes pane of each slide. Present from the notes view and you have the lesson plan in front of
you while the room sees only the big picture.

### The shape of every class deck

Classes 1 to 7 run the same spine, so the room learns the rhythm and a substitute can present cold:

Cover → Roadmap → Today → Show & Tell → Big idea → New blocks → Live demo → Spot the mistake →
Activity A → Activity B → Break → Today's goal → Blocks you'll need → Stuck? → Save →
Homework → Bonus → Next week

Class 8 is deliberately different, because the class is: Find the Bug, Playtest Swap, the polish
sprint and sharing, then the room resets and the showcase runs with families present.

**The roadmap slide is slide two of every deck.** It's the eight weekly checkpoints from
`curriculum/long-project-sky-catcher.md` written in the students' language — *says hello → you can
drive it → things fall → you can catch them → it keeps score → lives and Game Over → it gets
harder → you show your family* — with the weeks behind them marked done and the current one lit
up. It costs twenty seconds a class and it's what makes the term feel like one thing rather than
eight separate lessons.

**Every deck carries a Spot the mistake slide.** That's not filler — watching an adult be confused,
stay calm and work it out is the single most transferable thing in the course, and it needs to
happen every week.

## The volunteer briefing deck

https://claude.ai/artifact/XR7RoWXGWWMVXr3WYncB9K — 19 slides

Adult-facing, for the prep session before week one. Ratios and the arithmetic behind them, the
class rhythm, why twenty is the ceiling, what to do with spare volunteers, the skeleton files, the
five-year age spread, the seven-year-olds specifically, how to help on the floor without grabbing
the mouse, the no-grading rule, what to cut when time runs out, the week-eight timing problem, the
six-week prep timeline, and how we'll know it worked.

Allow about forty minutes with questions, and leave ten of them for the questions — that's where
you find out what people are actually worried about.

## Housekeeping

The decks are private to Ebby's Claude account until shared. Share each one from its Share menu
before another volunteer needs to open it. Each deck also downloads as PowerPoint or PDF, which is
worth doing before week one — venue wi-fi is not to be trusted.

## Keeping them in step with the curriculum

The slides were generated from the lesson plans in `curriculum/`. If you change a lesson plan —
a block, a number, a checkpoint — change the matching slide too, or the projector and the plan
will disagree in front of a room of children. The block colours follow Scratch's own palette,
darkened slightly where white text needed the contrast.
