# Committee and Promotions Brief

**As of 29 September 2026 · Ebby Peter**

## Summary

KCC Code Club is ready to run its first class, **Game Makers**, a volunteer-run coding class for
children aged 7 to 12, as a one-term pilot. It runs as eight weekly one-hour classes, then a
showcase where the children demo the game they built to their families. It costs families $40
for the term.

The course material is written and five volunteers have signed up. Nothing is booked yet.

- **From the committee:** five decisions before we set dates (below), chiefly the venue, a
  fee-waiver owner and the child-safety position.
- **From the promotions team:** a registration campaign once dates and venue are confirmed,
  built on the messages and details in this brief.

## The programme at a glance

Each child builds one arcade game, **Sky Catcher**, a layer at a time, and it works at the end
of every class.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Game Makers, the first class under the KCC Code Club umbrella |
| **Ages** | 7 to 12, no experience needed |
| **Format** | 8 weekly classes of one hour, then a showcase event for families |
| **Tool** | Scratch, the free block-based language from MIT, used in schools worldwide |
| **Class size** | Up to 16 children in the pilot |
| **Staffing** | Volunteers only, one per five children, never fewer than two adults in the room |
| **Fee** | $40 for the term ($5 a class), paid before the first class; no child turned away over it |
| **Laptops** | Families bring one. The club has none to lend this term |
| **Grading** | None. Homework is optional and never marked |

The material follows the Raspberry Pi Foundation's free Scratch pathways, and covers sequence,
events, loops, decisions, variables, messages and custom blocks. Future tracks may follow: Story
Makers (ScratchJr, youngest) and App Makers (Python, 12+).

## Decisions needed from the committee

Five decisions come before dates go out. The first three block everything else.

| # | Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Venue and dates.** Eight one-hour weekly slots, plus a slot for the showcase | Book the showcase with the term: a second hour straight after class 8, or time at the next KCC gathering. The showcase runs about 50 minutes for 15 children and doesn't fit inside a class |
| 2 | **Child safety.** We aren't registering with Code Club Aotearoa for the pilot, so their police-vetting requirement doesn't apply. KCC still needs to decide what it asks of adults working with its children | Agree a written position before dates are committed. Two adults in the room at all times is already a rule |
| 3 | **Consent and insurance.** Sign-off on the consent and photo-permission form, and confirmation that KCC's insurance covers the classes | Review the draft form we've prepared against KCC's policies and current NZ privacy requirements |
| 4 | **Fee waivers.** Who can quietly waive the $40, and on whose say-so | Name one person who can say yes without a meeting. A waiver that needs a meeting is a waiver nobody asks for |
| 5 | **The name in public.** "Code Club" is fine inside the KCC community, but we're not a registered Code Club. We're also considering dropping "Club" from the umbrella name | Settle the name before any promotion goes beyond KCC's own members |

Later, not now: sponsorship for four or five club laptops after the pilot. The number of families
this term's bring-your-own rule turns away is the case for it.

## Risks and how the pilot handles them

| Risk | What we're doing about it |
|---|---|
| **Bring-your-own laptop excludes the families who'd benefit most**, and they won't say that's why | Say it plainly in all promotion. Invite families without a laptop to tell us anyway. Count how many we turn away, as the case for sponsorship. A child who arrives without one shares with a neighbour for the day |
| **Children fall behind after a missed week and drop out** | A saved copy of the game at every week's stage, so anyone who misses a class starts the next one level |
| **An hour leaves little slack** | Each class has one short activity, so the time goes to the child's own game. Anything unfinished becomes optional homework |
| **Venue wi-fi fails at the showcase** | Every game backed up to a USB stick and runnable offline on the presenting laptop |
| **Volunteer absence** | Five volunteers for up to 16 children leaves spares. Every lesson plan is written so a substitute can run it cold |

## For the promotions team

The audience is **parents of 7 to 12-year-olds in the KCC community**. The message is simple:
your child builds a real game in eight weeks, and shows it to you at the end.

### Key messages

1. **They make something real.** Every child builds their own arcade game and demos it to their
   family at the showcase.
2. **No experience needed.** Built for complete beginners.
3. **Real foundations, taught through play.** Scratch is used in schools worldwide, and this is a
   genuine introduction to programming.
4. **Run by our own community.** Volunteers, small groups, at least two adults in the room.
5. **Nothing is graded.** No tests, no marking, no pressure.

### Details every piece must carry

- Ages 7 to 12
- [DAY] afternoons, [TIME], starting [DATE], at [VENUE]
- 8 weekly one-hour classes, plus a showcase on [SHOWCASE DATE]
- $40 for the term
- **Bring a laptop** (any from the last six or seven years, Chromebooks fine)
- [N] places, first come first served
- How to register: [LINK OR CONTACT]

### Claims to avoid

| Don't say | Because |
|---|---|
| Free | It's $40. Say quietly that no child is turned away over cost |
| Laptops provided | Families must bring one. Say so up front, so nobody finds out in week one |
| A Code Club Aotearoa club, or affiliated | We're not registered with them for the pilot |
| Anything about certificates, qualifications or school credit | There's a fun certificate at the showcase, and nothing formal |
| Specific dates, venue or class size | Until the committee confirms them |

**Headline:** Your child builds a game. In eight weeks. Then shows it to you.

rather know.

### Look and feel

- The KCC club logo, with the maroon, gold and green taken from it, as used across the course
  material.
- Fonts: Fredoka for headings, Nunito Sans for text (both free on Google Fonts).
- Friendly and plain. Talk to parents, not to children.
- **Photos:** only children whose consent form allows publishing. Until the first class, use
  screenshots of Scratch or the game rather than children.

## Timeline and next steps

teacher account, takes weeks to approve.

5. **Class 1**, then seven more weekly classes.
6. **Showcase** with families, then a short report back to the committee: how many finished, how
   many families the laptop rule turned away, and whether to run term two.
