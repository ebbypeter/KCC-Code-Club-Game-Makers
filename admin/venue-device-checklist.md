# Venue and Device Checklist

## The venue

### Test before you commit

- [ ] **Wi-fi with fifteen devices actually connected.** Not "the venue has wi-fi". Take five
      devices down there and stream something on all of them. Community hall wi-fi is very often
      one consumer router with a fair-use cap, and it falls over at exactly ten connections
- [ ] **Power.** Count the sockets. Fifteen laptops on battery for 90 minutes is usually fine,
      but you'll want six or eight sockets and a couple of multi-boxes for the ones that arrive
      at 12%
- [ ] **Projector or a big screen**, and whether your laptop can actually drive it. Bring your
      own adapter — the venue's will be the wrong one
- [ ] **Tables at a height children can type at**, and enough of them. Laptops on laps for
      90 minutes is miserable
- [ ] **Lighting and glare.** Afternoon sun on fifteen screens ruins a session. Check at the
      time of day you'll actually be running
- [ ] **A wall or whiteboard** for the block lists and working through examples
- [ ] Toilets, and the walk to them
- [ ] Somewhere for parents to wait, if they do

### Room layout

Arrange tables so **you can walk behind every single screen**. This is the single most important
layout decision and it's usually got wrong.

Rows facing the projector look tidy and are terrible — you can't see what anyone is doing. A
horseshoe, or clusters of four with gaps between them, means the floater can see every screen
from the middle of the room without leaning over anyone.

Being able to glance at fifteen screens and spot the one that hasn't changed in five minutes is
how you catch the quietly stuck child. Design for that.

## Devices

### The BYOD problem

A "bring your own laptop" requirement will quietly exclude the families who'd benefit most, and
they will not tell you that's why they didn't sign up. They'll just not sign up.

Plan for a mix: ask on the registration form, then source spares to cover the gap. Four or five
club laptops covers most groups. Sources worth trying: club members' old machines, a member's
workplace refreshing its fleet, a local school, a library, or a community tech recycler.

Say clearly on the parent letter that borrowing a laptop needs no explanation. Make it the
easiest box to tick.

### Minimum spec

Scratch is not demanding, but it is a browser app and it wants a bit of memory.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Anything modern** | Fine — any laptop from the last six or seven years |
| **RAM** | 4GB works, 8GB is comfortable |
| **Browser** | Current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari |
| **Screen** | 11" minimum, honestly. Smaller than that and the block palette plus the stage plus the code area don't fit together |
| **Mouse** | **Bring spare USB mice.** Dragging blocks on a trackpad is genuinely harder, and for an 8-year-old it's the difference between building things and giving up. This is the highest-value $15 you'll spend |

### Tablets and Chromebooks

- **Chromebooks:** fine. Scratch runs in the browser.
- **iPads and Android tablets:** Scratch works but drag-and-drop on a touchscreen is fiddly for
  the more complex scripts, and My Blocks in week seven are painful. Workable if it's all a
  family has; not what you'd choose.
- **Phones:** no.

### Before every session

- [ ] All club laptops charged
- [ ] All logged into Scratch already
- [ ] One browser tab open on the Scratch editor. Not fifteen tabs, not a games site
- [ ] Spare mice out on the tables
- [ ] Scratch Desktop installed as the offline fallback (see `scratch-accounts-setup.md`)

## When the internet dies

It will. Have this ready and it's a shrug instead of a cancelled session.

1. **Scratch Desktop is already installed** on every club machine. Projects save locally.
2. **The USB stick** has last week's `.sb3` files for every student, backed up at the end of
   every session.
3. Students open their `.sb3` in Scratch Desktop, work normally, and save back to the stick.
4. Upload to their online accounts next week when the wi-fi is behaving.

The whole recovery takes about five minutes if the backups exist, and the session is
unsalvageable if they don't. Do the backups.

## Consumables

Per term, roughly:

- Name stickers for week one
- Printed handouts — 15 copies × 8 weeks
- Pencils and a few pens
- Blu-tack for putting the block lists on the wall
- A whiteboard marker that actually works. Bring your own; the venue's are always dry
- Certificates for week eight
- Snacks for the showcase
